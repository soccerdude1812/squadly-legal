Squadly Coach

Privacy Policy

Effective [RELEASE DAY: the date 2.0 goes live]  ·  Published by Parle LLC

This policy covers the Squadly app for iPhone and Android and the website heysquadly.com.

Squadly is an app for youth-soccer coaches for tracking playing time and managing lineups. The app is free. Talking to Squadly, its assistant, is free for a team’s first three games and then needs Squadly Pro.

What Squadly stores

About you: your name, email, and an account ID. For Squadly’s assistant, a count for each day of how many requests you made and how much of the AI and speech services they used, so one account can’t use up what everyone shares. The iPhone app also notes each time you ask Squadly something: when, for which team and game, and how it ended, such as whether you accepted it. We count how you use Squadly to see whether it helps coaches, never what you said. The app also makes a random ID for the phone it’s installed on, and a game records the ID of the phone that runs its clock, so the team’s other phones know which one is running the game.

About your team: what you enter: player names, jersey numbers, playing time, attendance, lineups, and game history; parent contact info if you choose to add it; and player photos, which stay on the phone you add them on. Coaches on the same team can see each other’s email address. An invite link carries the email of the coach who sent it.

If you connect TeamSnap in the iPhone app or on heysquadly.com, we keep the connection on our server (TeamSnap’s access key, encrypted) and check TeamSnap about every 5 minutes, so changes there reach your phone and heysquadly.com. We keep a copy of only what Squadly uses: each team member’s TeamSnap ID, name and shirt number, and whether they’re a manager or not a player; each event’s name, whether it’s a game, its location, opponent and start time; and who’s coming to each event. A member’s email is kept only when TeamSnap sends a single address (today it sends lists, so none is kept). Nothing else is kept: no addresses, birthdays, gender, phone numbers or invitation codes. Disconnecting TeamSnap or deleting your account removes both.

If you subscribe to Squadly Pro: whoever sells it takes the payment, and we never see your card. In the iPhone app that is Apple, on Android it is Google Play, and on heysquadly.com it is Stripe, which sells it for us through its Link service and handles the sales tax. Your account ID goes with the purchase so the seller can tell us whose it is, and Stripe also gets your email address. The seller sends us a record of the subscription (which plan, when it started and ends, whether it renews, whether it was cancelled or refunded, and the seller’s number for it), and we keep it with your account so the other coaches on your team are covered too. If your team uses a region’s code, we keep which team used it, who entered it and when, and we note each try at a code so codes can’t be guessed. We also count how many of each team’s games have started, for the three free games.

Squadly does not collect location, contacts, or browsing history.

What we never do

No ads, no tracking or analytics SDKs, no selling or sharing data for marketing, and nothing you enter is used to train AI. Your data is used only to run Squadly for you and your co-coaches.

Squadly’s assistant

Ask Squadly for something long (“make our lineup, start Maya, and balance the minutes”) and it works through it with an AI model run by Mistral AI, a company based in France. Squadly sends only what the job needs: your words, shirt numbers, positions, minutes played (today and in past games), results, and your schedule. Every player on your roster has their name swapped for a made-up one before anything leaves your phone or browser, and so does a new player you ask Squadly to add. A name that isn’t on your roster, like an opposing player, goes as you said it, so keep children’s names out of requests when you can. The assistant’s changes wait for you to tap Accept; the phone’s own short commands (subs, goals, the clock) carry out after a moment unless you cancel.

Files you give Squadly on heysquadly.com (a roster, a schedule, a calendar, a screenshot, or a table you paste) are read on your own computer, in your browser, and are never uploaded. Screenshots are read by a text reader that runs in your browser too. Squadly takes from a file only what the app keeps: players’ names, shirt numbers and positions; game days, kickoffs and opponents; and who’s coming. Parents’ names, emails, phone numbers, addresses, birthdays, medical notes and registration numbers are left out. If you just add a file, no AI is involved. If you ask Squadly to do something with it, Mistral sees those rows with every child’s name swapped for a made-up one, including children not on your roster yet, and the file’s name with any names taken out. Nothing from a file is saved until you tap Accept.

Cloud hearing. Squadly listens only when you press its button, until you let go or finish talking. Each time you talk to it, the audio is sent as a short clip, with the first names on your roster and the opponent’s name so they’re spelled right, to a speech-to-text service run by Groq, a company based in the United States, which sends the words back. Names you say out loud are in that audio, so a child’s name can reach Groq this way. To keep audio off the internet, type instead of talking. Typed requests that Squadly’s assistant takes on still go to Mistral, with player names swapped.

When you tell Squadly which player it misheard (“did you mean Maya?”), it remembers that on your phone so it hears the name right next time. That stays on your phone.

Neither service uses any of this to train AI. Each may keep it for up to 30 days only to check for abuse and keep its service running, then it is deleted. Squadly keeps none of it.

Where it lives and who can see it

Data is stored with Supabase (a hosted database service) on servers in the United States and encrypted in transit. Signing in on heysquadly.com shows the same data, under the same rules. Only the coaches on your team can see your team’s data, enforced by database-level security rules. There are no public pages or shareable links to player data.

About players

Players never use Squadly: no logins, no screens for them. The coach decides what to enter and is responsible for any consent their league or parents require, especially for photos. Roster data is shown only to the team’s coaches. Parents: to see or remove your child’s info, ask the coach, or email eeshankhandelwal123@gmail.com.

Deleting your data

Settings › Account › Delete Account in the app, or Account › Delete account on heysquadly.com. Deletion is immediate and permanent: your account and TeamSnap connection are erased. A team you head-coach alone is erased entirely: roster, games, everything. A team with other coaches passes to the coach who joined it first, with its data, and the app shows you who before you confirm. Teams you assist on remove you as a member. A Squadly Pro subscription bought on heysquadly.com is cancelled at once and your customer record at Stripe is deleted; Stripe keeps its own payment records. One bought from Apple or Google Play keeps renewing until you cancel it in that store. If deletion fails, email eeshankhandelwal123@gmail.com.

Removing a player in the Roster tab takes them off the roster for every coach on the team. Their name, past minutes and any parent contact you entered stay hidden in the team’s history, so season totals stay right, until the team is deleted. Email eeshankhandelwal123@gmail.com and we’ll erase them completely.

How long we keep it. Nothing is deleted on a schedule: data stays until you or your team’s coaches delete it, or your account is deleted as above. Contact messages are kept until you ask us to delete them; deleting your account only detaches your account ID from them. A TeamSnap copy is kept until you disconnect TeamSnap or delete your account. A removed player stays hidden with the team until the team is deleted or you ask us to erase them.

The website, heysquadly.com

You can sign in there with the same Squadly account you use in the app, with Apple, Google or an email link. Signing in lets you see and plan your team the same as in the app, post in Team Talk (free with every Squadly account), file and vote on requests, and comment. Signed-out visitors can read Team Talk and the requests and send a contact message; nothing else on the site works without an account.

Team Talk and the requests are public: anyone can read the posts and comments, signed in or not, and each post shows your display name and not your account ID. Keep players’ names out of them. Posts, comments and votes are stored against your account. Your display name is made from your first name and last initial, never your full name or email, and you can change it at any time. Vote counts are public; who voted is not. Replies to your posts create a notification for you inside the site.

The contact form stores the name, email address and message you send, and your account ID if you were signed in, so we can reply. Messages are kept, including ones marked as spam, and each one that isn’t spam is copied to Eeshan’s to-do list so he sees it.

The site sets cookies only to keep you signed in, to remember which of your teams you are looking at and your time zone, and, for an hour while you connect TeamSnap, to hold that connection. Your browser also keeps a few display choices (whether Squadly’s panel is open, a hint you closed) and, once you add a screenshot, the text reader’s English model, so the next one reads faster; none of it is personal data. There are no analytics or advertising trackers. Apple’s sign-in script loads on the sign-in page. The support page shows a preview image of the walkthrough video that loads from YouTube; opening the video takes you to YouTube, which has its own privacy policy.

The site is hosted by Vercel. Website data lives in the same Supabase project as the app, on servers in the United States. Deleting your Squadly account removes your profile, posts, comments and votes there too. Contact messages are kept so they can be answered; your account ID is detached from them when the account is deleted.
